\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
EGP-NPA combines an equivariant residual policy, local dynamic allocation, and a completion envelope for multi-robot rearrangement under coupled safety constraints. The seven-robot certificate identifies limitations of error-monotone control on safely reachable tasks. The two-node subproblem explains allocation-induced yielding, while the envelope gives first-order residual suppression and restores the zero-velocity goal equilibrium.

One full-budget checkpoint safely completes $96$ development and $48$ held-out tasks. Eight budget-matched training runs and $384$ evaluations show that the arrival effect of projection regularization depends on critic configuration: it reduces arrivals with team critics and increases them with local critics. Static allocation is reported as a fixed-weight intervention, separating certification and computational-budget stops from zero-speed stalls. We additionally train the official DGPPO update in two budget-controlled replicates and evaluate it on new tasks, reporting outcomes and adaptation differences in Table~\ref{tab:external}. Internal ablations are no longer substituted for external algorithms.

Each learning-variant cell currently has only two training replicates. Main single-integrator evaluations use exact spectral features, whereas physical experiments separately incorporate online estimation. Each graph layer communicates over one-hop edges, and multilayer inference has a larger information receptive field. Physical simulations exhibit both distance violations and nonarrival under conservative compensation. Independent-method comparisons require faithful algorithm implementations, shared task adaptations, comparable total interaction and tuning budgets, additional training replicates, and new held-out tasks.
